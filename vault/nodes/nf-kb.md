# NF-kB (Nuclear Factor Kappa-Light-Chain-Enhancer of Activated B Cells)

Nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-kB) is a family of structurally related transcription factors that serve as master regulators of the innate and adaptive immune responses, inflammation, cell survival, and proliferation. The mammalian NF-kB family comprises five members: RelA (p65), RelB, c-Rel, NF-kB1 (p105/p50), and NF-kB2 (p100/p52), all sharing a conserved Rel homology domain (RHD) responsible for DNA binding, dimerization, and interaction with the inhibitory IkB proteins. These subunits form homo- and heterodimers, with the p65/p50 heterodimer being the most abundant and transcriptionally active form in most cell types. In resting cells, NF-kB dimers are sequestered in the cytoplasm by inhibitory IkB proteins (IkB-alpha, IkB-beta, IkB-epsilon), which mask the nuclear localization signal (NLS) and prevent nuclear translocation.

NF-kB is arguably the most important transcription factor in inflammation and immunity, controlling the expression of over 500 target genes including pro-inflammatory cytokines ([[il-6]], [[il-1beta]], [[tnf-alpha]]), chemokines (IL-8, MCP-1, RANTES), adhesion molecules (ICAM-1, VCAM-1, E-selectin), inflammatory enzymes ([[cox-2]], iNOS), and anti-apoptotic proteins (Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, cIAP). The NF-kB signaling pathway is activated within minutes of encountering pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), or pro-inflammatory cytokines, making it one of the most rapid transcriptional responses available to the cell.

## Upstream Causes

NF-kB activation occurs through two principal pathways: the canonical (classical) pathway and the non-canonical (alternative) pathway. The canonical pathway is the primary pro-inflammatory route and is activated by pattern recognition receptors (Toll-like receptors, especially TLR4 responding to LPS), pro-inflammatory cytokines (most importantly [[tnf-alpha]] via TNFR1 and [[il-1beta]] via IL-1R1), antigen receptors (TCR, BCR), and various stress signals. These stimuli converge on the IkB kinase (IKK) complex, composed of catalytic subunits IKK-alpha and IKK-beta and the regulatory subunit NEMO (IKK-gamma). Activated IKK-beta phosphorylates IkB-alpha at serines 32 and 36, marking it for K48-linked polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. The liberated p65/p50 dimer then translocates to the nucleus and binds kB sites (consensus: GGGRNWYYCC) in target gene promoters. [[tnf-alpha]] drives NF-kB activation through TRADD/TRAF2/RIP1 signaling, while [[il-1beta]] signals through MyD88/IRAK/TRAF6. Importantly, many NF-kB target genes (e.g., [[tnf-alpha]], [[il-1beta]]) are themselves NF-kB activators, creating positive feedback loops that can amplify and sustain inflammatory responses.

The most critical negative regulator of NF-kB in the context of the stress response is the [[cortisol]]-[[gr]] complex. The [[gr]], upon activation by [[cortisol]], inhibits NF-kB through multiple mechanisms: direct protein-protein interaction between GR and the p65 subunit (transrepression/tethering), induction of IkB-alpha transcription (which re-sequesters NF-kB in the cytoplasm), induction of GILZ (which inhibits NF-kB nuclear translocation), and competition for limiting quantities of shared coactivators (CBP/p300).

## Downstream Effects

NF-kB drives transcription of a vast array of inflammatory and immune response genes. Among the most important targets in the context of the [[innate-immune-response]] are the pro-inflammatory cytokines [[il-6]], [[il-1beta]], and [[tnf-alpha]], which collectively orchestrate the acute phase response, leukocyte recruitment, and systemic inflammatory signaling. NF-kB also directly transactivates [[cox-2]] (PTGS2), the inducible enzyme that converts arachidonic acid to prostaglandins PGE2 and PGI2, driving pain, fever, vasodilation, and inflammatory edema. Through its induction of [[il-6]], [[il-1beta]], and [[tnf-alpha]], NF-kB indirectly stimulates the [[hpa-axis]] via cytokine-driven [[crh]] release from hypothalamic PVN neurons, establishing the immune-to-neuroendocrine arm of the inflammatory reflex. This NF-kB-driven cytokine production ultimately triggers [[cortisol]] release, which feeds back to inhibit NF-kB, forming a critical homeostatic circuit: the inflammatory-glucocorticoid negative feedback loop. NF-kB also promotes cell survival through induction of anti-apoptotic genes, promotes leukocyte adhesion and migration through adhesion molecule expression, and contributes to the adaptive immune response through regulation of MHC class I/II, co-stimulatory molecules, and immunoglobulin class switching.

## Holon Context

Within the [[innate-immune-response]] holon, NF-kB is the central transcriptional hub that converts pathogen detection and danger signals into a coordinated gene expression program. It sits at the convergence point of multiple upstream pattern recognition and cytokine receptor pathways and drives the divergent transcriptional outputs (cytokines, chemokines, adhesion molecules, enzymes) that collectively constitute the inflammatory response. NF-kB's positive feedback loops (its own target genes [[tnf-alpha]] and [[il-1beta]] reactivate NF-kB) create the potential for self-amplifying inflammation, while its negative regulation by the [[cortisol]]-[[gr]] axis provides the essential brake that prevents inflammatory runaway. This NF-kB/GR antagonism represents one of the most fundamental regulatory axes in mammalian physiology, connecting the [[innate-immune-response]] holon directly to the [[hpa-axis]] and [[glucocorticoid-feedback]] holons. NF-kB thus functions as a bidirectional bridge: it sends signals from the immune system to the neuroendocrine system (via cytokine-driven [[crh]] release) and receives signals from the neuroendocrine system (via [[cortisol]]-[[gr]] transrepression).

## Clinical Relevance

Constitutive or excessive NF-kB activation is a hallmark of chronic inflammatory diseases, autoimmune disorders, and many cancers. In rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, asthma, and psoriasis, persistent NF-kB activation in immune and stromal cells drives ongoing production of [[tnf-alpha]], [[il-6]], [[il-1beta]], and [[cox-2]], perpetuating tissue damage and disease progression. The therapeutic success of anti-[[tnf-alpha]] biologics (infliximab, adalimumab, etanercept), IL-6 receptor antagonists (tocilizumab), and IL-1 antagonists (anakinra, canakinumab) can be understood mechanistically as interruption of NF-kB positive feedback loops. Glucocorticoid therapy (dexamethasone, prednisone) achieves its anti-inflammatory effects largely through [[gr]]-mediated NF-kB suppression. In oncology, constitutive NF-kB activation promotes tumor survival, proliferation, angiogenesis, and metastasis, and is a feature of many hematological malignancies (diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, multiple myeloma, Hodgkin lymphoma) and solid tumors. Gain-of-function mutations in upstream NF-kB pathway components (CARD11, MYD88, MALT1) are oncogenic drivers. Direct NF-kB inhibitors have proven challenging to develop clinically due to the pathway's essential role in normal immunity, but proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib) achieve partial NF-kB suppression by blocking IkB-alpha degradation and are effective in multiple myeloma.
