# 11beta-HSD2 (11-Beta-Hydroxysteroid Dehydrogenase Type 2)

11-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 (11beta-HSD2), encoded by the HSD11B2 gene on chromosome 16q22.1, is a 405-amino-acid NAD+-dependent enzyme that catalyzes the unidirectional conversion of biologically active [[cortisol]] (11-beta-hydroxyl group) to inactive cortisone (11-keto group). Unlike [[11beta-hsd1]], which is a bidirectional reductase favoring cortisol regeneration, 11beta-HSD2 is an exclusive dehydrogenase with no significant reductase activity under physiological conditions. This strict unidirectionality is enforced by the enzyme's use of NAD+ as a cofactor (rather than NADP+/NADPH) and the high NAD+/NADH ratio in the cellular compartment where 11beta-HSD2 resides. The enzyme is a type II transmembrane protein localized to the endoplasmic reticulum, with its catalytic domain facing the cytoplasm.

11beta-HSD2 serves a critical "gatekeeper" function in aldosterone-target tissues by preventing [[cortisol]] from activating the [[mr]]. This protection is necessary because [[cortisol]] circulates at concentrations 100-1000 fold higher than aldosterone, yet both hormones bind [[mr]] with comparable high affinity. Without 11beta-HSD2 activity, [[cortisol]] would constitutively saturate [[mr]] in the kidney, colon, and other mineralocorticoid-responsive epithelia, rendering aldosterone signaling irrelevant and abolishing the physiological regulation of sodium and potassium homeostasis by the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system. The expression pattern of 11beta-HSD2 is correspondingly restricted to tissues where aldosterone selectivity is required: the distal nephron (principal cells of the cortical collecting duct), distal colon, salivary glands, and sweat glands, as well as the placenta and fetal tissues.

## Upstream Causes

11beta-HSD2 expression is constitutive in its target tissues and is not acutely regulated by stress or HPA axis activation, reflecting its role as a constitutive protective barrier rather than a dynamically regulated enzyme. However, several factors influence HSD11B2 expression. The mineralocorticoid aldosterone has been shown to upregulate 11beta-HSD2 expression in renal collecting duct cells, reinforcing its own selectivity at the [[mr]]. Epigenetic silencing of HSD11B2 through promoter methylation has been observed in certain cancers and in response to dietary factors, potentially leading to local cortisol excess and inappropriate [[mr]] activation. Placental HSD11B2 expression is critical during pregnancy for protecting the fetus from maternal [[cortisol]] exposure, and its expression is regulated by placental growth factors and oxygen tension. Inflammatory cytokines, including [[tnf-alpha]] and [[il-1beta]], can downregulate 11beta-HSD2 expression in certain contexts, which may contribute to local cortisol excess and enhanced anti-inflammatory signaling during inflammation. The substrate for 11beta-HSD2 is [[cortisol]] itself, whose availability is determined by the upstream [[hpa-axis]] ([[crh]], [[acth]], [[mc2r]]) and by [[11beta-hsd1]]-mediated cortisol regeneration in neighboring cells.

## Downstream Effects

The principal downstream effect of 11beta-HSD2 is the local inactivation of [[cortisol]] to cortisone, thereby preventing [[cortisol]] from occupying and activating the [[mr]] in aldosterone-target tissues. This enzymatic shielding allows aldosterone to function as the selective [[mr]] agonist in the distal nephron, where [[mr]] activation drives sodium reabsorption via ENaC and ROMK-mediated potassium excretion. When 11beta-HSD2 is absent or inhibited, [[cortisol]] saturates renal [[mr]], producing a state of apparent mineralocorticoid excess: sodium retention, volume expansion, hypertension, hypokalemia, and metabolic alkalosis, all occurring in the setting of suppressed aldosterone and renin. In the placenta, 11beta-HSD2 converts maternal [[cortisol]] to cortisone before it reaches the fetal circulation, protecting the developing fetus from excessive glucocorticoid exposure that could impair growth, alter brain development, and program long-term cardiometabolic risk. Cortisone generated by 11beta-HSD2 is not simply a waste product; it enters the circulation and serves as the substrate for [[11beta-hsd1]] in tissues such as the liver and adipose, where it is converted back to active [[cortisol]], forming a systemic cortisol-cortisone shuttle.

## Holon Context

Within the [[glucocorticoid-feedback]] holon, 11beta-HSD2 operates as the inactivation arm of the cortisol-cortisone shuttle, functioning in metabolic opposition to [[11beta-hsd1]]. This enzyme pair creates a tissue-autonomous regulatory layer that modulates effective glucocorticoid tone below the systemic level set by the [[hpa-axis]]. The restricted expression of 11beta-HSD2 in aldosterone-target tissues and the placenta illustrates the principle of context-dependent signal interpretation: the same circulating hormone ([[cortisol]]) is rendered biologically silent in some tissues (where 11beta-HSD2 is expressed) while remaining fully active in others (where only [[11beta-hsd1]] is present), enabling tissue-specific responses to a shared systemic signal. In the broader multi-scale architecture, 11beta-HSD2 exemplifies how local enzymatic activity can override systemic hormonal control, allowing nested holons (tissue-level regulation within system-level control) to maintain distinct functional identities.

## Clinical Relevance

Loss-of-function mutations in HSD11B2 cause the syndrome of apparent mineralocorticoid excess (AME, OMIM #218030), a rare autosomal recessive disorder presenting in childhood with severe hypertension, hypokalemia, low renin, and low aldosterone. The pathophysiology is straightforward: without 11beta-HSD2 to inactivate [[cortisol]] in the kidney, [[cortisol]] constitutively activates renal [[mr]], producing sodium retention and potassium wasting indistinguishable from primary aldosteronism except for the suppressed aldosterone levels. Treatment involves MR antagonists (spironolactone), potassium supplementation, and sodium restriction. The same pathophysiological mechanism is recapitulated by pharmacological or dietary inhibition of 11beta-HSD2: glycyrrhetinic acid and its precursor glycyrrhizin (found in natural liquorice) are potent 11beta-HSD2 inhibitors, and chronic liquorice consumption is a well-recognized cause of hypertension and hypokalemia. Carbenoxolone, a synthetic glycyrrhetinic acid derivative historically used for peptic ulcers, also inhibits 11beta-HSD2. Reduced placental 11beta-HSD2 expression has been associated with intrauterine growth restriction, preeclampsia, and fetal programming of adult hypertension and metabolic disease -- a mechanism central to the Barker hypothesis of developmental origins of adult disease. Epigenetic downregulation of HSD11B2 has been observed in certain renal cell carcinomas, potentially contributing to a paraneoplastic hypertension syndrome.
