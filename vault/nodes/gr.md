# GR (Glucocorticoid Receptor)

The glucocorticoid receptor (GR, NR3C1) is a 777-amino-acid ligand-activated transcription factor belonging to the nuclear receptor superfamily. Encoded by the NR3C1 gene on chromosome 5q31.3, the GR protein contains the canonical nuclear receptor domain architecture: an N-terminal transactivation domain (AF-1), a central zinc-finger DNA-binding domain (DBD), a hinge region, and a C-terminal ligand-binding domain (LBD) containing the AF-2 transactivation surface. Alternative splicing of the NR3C1 gene generates multiple isoforms, the most studied being GR-alpha (the transcriptionally active form) and GR-beta (a dominant-negative isoform that cannot bind ligand and inhibits GR-alpha activity). Alternative translation initiation from eight AUG codons in exon 2 produces additional N-terminal variants (GR-A through GR-D) with differing transcriptional activities, contributing to the cell-type-specific diversity of glucocorticoid responses.

In its unliganded state, GR resides in the cytoplasm as part of a multiprotein chaperone complex that includes heat shock protein 90 (Hsp90), Hsp70, and immunophilins (FKBP51 and FKBP52). Hsp90 maintains the LBD in a conformation competent for [[cortisol]] binding, while FKBP51 reduces and FKBP52 enhances GR nuclear translocation efficiency. Upon [[cortisol]] binding, a conformational change displaces FKBP51 and recruits FKBP52, which facilitates dynein-mediated transport of the ligand-receptor complex along microtubules to the nucleus. The GR binds [[cortisol]] with a Kd of approximately 30 nM, roughly 10-fold lower affinity than the [[mr]], meaning that GR is preferentially activated during stress-induced [[cortisol]] elevations that exceed basal levels.

## Upstream Causes

GR activation is determined primarily by the availability of its ligand, [[cortisol]], whose circulating concentrations are governed by the [[hpa-axis]] cascade: [[crh]] stimulates [[acth]], which drives adrenal [[cortisol]] synthesis via [[mc2r]]. The effective intracellular concentration of [[cortisol]] available for GR binding is further modulated by the pre-receptor enzymes [[11beta-hsd1]] (which regenerates active [[cortisol]] from cortisone, amplifying GR activation in tissues such as liver and adipose) and [[11beta-hsd2]] (which inactivates [[cortisol]] to cortisone, protecting [[mr]] from cortisol occupancy in kidney and placenta). GR expression itself is regulated by glucocorticoid autoregulation: chronic [[cortisol]] exposure downregulates GR mRNA and protein levels, a mechanism contributing to glucocorticoid resistance. Epigenetic modifications of the NR3C1 promoter, particularly DNA methylation at the exon 1F promoter region, are influenced by early-life experience and maternal care, establishing long-lasting individual differences in GR expression and HPA axis reactivity. The GR-beta isoform, upregulated by pro-inflammatory cytokines including [[tnf-alpha]] and [[il-6]], functions as a dominant-negative inhibitor of GR-alpha, providing a mechanism by which inflammation can induce glucocorticoid resistance.

## Downstream Effects

GR mediates its genomic effects through two principal mechanisms. In transactivation, GR homodimers bind to palindromic glucocorticoid response elements (GREs, consensus sequence GGTACAnnnTGTTCT) in the promoter and enhancer regions of target genes, recruiting coactivator complexes (SRC/p160 family, CBP/p300) to drive transcription of anti-inflammatory and metabolic genes. Key transactivated genes include ANXA1 (annexin A1, which inhibits phospholipase A2 and thus reduces substrate availability for [[cox-2]]), DUSP1/MKP-1 (which dephosphorylates and inactivates MAP kinases), GILZ (glucocorticoid-induced leucine zipper), TSC22D3, and NFKBIA (IkB-alpha, the endogenous inhibitor of [[nf-kb]]). In transrepression, the GR monomer physically interacts with pro-inflammatory transcription factors -- most importantly [[nf-kb]] (p65/RelA subunit) and AP-1 (c-Jun/c-Fos) -- through direct protein-protein interactions (tethering), preventing their binding to DNA or their recruitment of coactivators. This transrepression mechanism is responsible for the suppression of hundreds of [[nf-kb]]-dependent inflammatory genes, including [[il-6]], [[il-1beta]], [[tnf-alpha]], and [[cox-2]]. The relative contribution of transactivation versus transrepression to the anti-inflammatory effects of glucocorticoids has been intensively debated, and current evidence suggests both mechanisms are essential.

At the systems level, GR mediates negative feedback within the [[hpa-axis]]: in hypothalamic PVN neurons, GR activation suppresses [[crh]] gene transcription; in anterior pituitary corticotrophs, it inhibits POMC transcription and [[acth]] secretion. GR in hippocampal neurons modulates synaptic plasticity, memory consolidation, and inhibitory tone on the HPA axis.

## Holon Context

The GR occupies a uniquely cross-cutting position across multiple holons. Within the [[hpa-axis]] holon, GR is the molecular sensor that detects the output signal ([[cortisol]]) and translates it into negative feedback at the hypothalamic and pituitary levels, closing the regulatory loop. Within the [[glucocorticoid-feedback]] holon, GR operates in a complementary relationship with [[mr]]: [[mr]] (high affinity, saturated at basal [[cortisol]]) mediates tonic, permissive effects and proactive feedback, while GR (lower affinity, recruited at stress-level [[cortisol]]) mediates reactive feedback and emergency anti-inflammatory responses. This dual-receptor system allows the organism to distinguish between basal and stress-associated glucocorticoid signaling using a single ligand. Within the [[innate-immune-response]] holon, GR is the principal molecular brake on inflammatory gene expression, directly antagonizing [[nf-kb]] to constrain the amplitude and duration of cytokine production by [[il-6]], [[il-1beta]], [[tnf-alpha]], and [[cox-2]].

## Clinical Relevance

GR dysfunction and glucocorticoid resistance are implicated in a wide range of disorders. Primary generalized glucocorticoid resistance (Chrousos syndrome) is caused by inactivating NR3C1 mutations and manifests with elevated [[cortisol]] and [[acth]] but without Cushingoid features, instead presenting with hypertension, hypokalemia, and hyperandrogenism due to [[acth]]-driven adrenal androgen and mineralocorticoid excess. Acquired glucocorticoid resistance, driven by inflammation-induced GR-beta upregulation, reduced GR expression, or altered GR phosphorylation, contributes to steroid-refractory asthma, inflammatory bowel disease, and rheumatoid arthritis. The pharmacological exploitation of GR remains the cornerstone of anti-inflammatory therapy, with synthetic glucocorticoids (dexamethasone, prednisolone, budesonide) designed for enhanced GR binding and reduced mineralocorticoid activity. The ongoing search for dissociated GR ligands (selective GR modulators, SEGRMs) that preferentially activate transrepression over transactivation aims to separate anti-inflammatory efficacy from metabolic side effects, though clinically successful SEGRMs remain elusive. FKBP5 polymorphisms that modulate GR sensitivity have been associated with post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, and altered stress reactivity, linking GR biology to psychiatric genetics.
