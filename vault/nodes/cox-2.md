# COX-2 (Cyclooxygenase-2)

Cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), also known as prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 2 (PTGS2), is a 72-kDa homodimeric enzyme encoded by the PTGS2 gene on chromosome 1q31.1. COX-2 catalyzes the rate-limiting step in the conversion of arachidonic acid to prostaglandin H2 (PGH2), which is subsequently converted by tissue-specific prostaglandin synthases to the biologically active prostanoids: prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), prostacyclin (PGI2), prostaglandin D2 (PGD2), prostaglandin F2-alpha (PGF2a), and thromboxane A2 (TXA2). The enzyme possesses two distinct catalytic activities: a cyclooxygenase (bis-dioxygenase) activity that converts arachidonic acid to PGG2, and a peroxidase activity that reduces PGG2 to PGH2. COX-2 is a monotopic membrane protein localized to the endoplasmic reticulum and nuclear envelope, with its active site facing the membrane interior, where it accesses arachidonic acid released from membrane phospholipids.

COX-2 is the inducible isoform of cyclooxygenase, in contrast to COX-1 (PTGS1), which is constitutively expressed in most tissues and maintains homeostatic prostanoid production for gastric cytoprotection, platelet aggregation, and renal blood flow. Under basal conditions, COX-2 expression is low or absent in most cell types, but it is rapidly and dramatically induced (10-80 fold) by inflammatory stimuli, making it a defining marker of the inflammatory response. COX-2 shares 61% amino acid identity with COX-1 and has an almost identical catalytic mechanism, but its larger active-site channel (due to a valine-to-isoleucine substitution at position 523) allows selective inhibition by COX-2-selective inhibitors (coxibs) and also permits metabolism of a broader range of fatty acid substrates, including endocannabinoids.

## Upstream Causes

COX-2 transcription is driven primarily by [[nf-kb]], with additional contributions from CREB, C/EBP, AP-1, and NFAT transcription factors. The PTGS2 promoter contains well-characterized binding sites for [[nf-kb]] (two kB elements), a CRE site, a C/EBP site, and an E-box, enabling integration of diverse inflammatory signals. Pro-inflammatory cytokines [[tnf-alpha]], [[il-1beta]], and [[il-6]] are potent inducers of COX-2 expression through their activation of [[nf-kb]] and MAP kinase pathways. Pattern recognition receptor signaling (LPS via TLR4, peptidoglycan via TLR2) rapidly induces COX-2 transcription, and the resulting PGE2 production occurs within 2-4 hours of inflammatory stimulus. Growth factors (EGF, PDGF), oncogenes (Ras, Src), and hypoxia (via HIF-1alpha) also induce COX-2, linking inflammation to tumorigenesis. Post-transcriptional regulation is important: the PTGS2 mRNA contains AU-rich elements in its 3'-UTR that promote mRNA destabilization, and the RNA-binding protein HuR stabilizes COX-2 mRNA in inflammatory conditions.

The substrate for COX-2 is arachidonic acid, which must be released from membrane phospholipids by phospholipase A2 (primarily cytosolic PLA2-alpha, cPLA2). This substrate supply represents a critical regulatory point: [[cortisol]], acting through [[gr]], induces annexin A1 (lipocortin-1), which inhibits cPLA2 activity and thereby reduces arachidonic acid availability for COX-2. [[cortisol]]-[[gr]] also directly suppresses COX-2 transcription through [[nf-kb]] transrepression and through induction of MKP-1, which dephosphorylates p38 MAPK required for COX-2 mRNA stabilization. [[il-10]] also suppresses COX-2 expression via STAT3-mediated mechanisms.

## Downstream Effects

The primary downstream product of COX-2 activity is prostaglandin H2 (PGH2), which is the common precursor for all prostanoids. In inflammatory contexts, PGH2 is converted predominantly to PGE2 by microsomal prostaglandin E synthase-1 (mPGES-1, also an inducible enzyme co-induced with COX-2), and to PGI2 by prostacyclin synthase. PGE2 is the principal mediator of inflammation-associated pain, fever, and vasodilation: it sensitizes peripheral nociceptors through EP1 and EP3 receptors (inflammatory pain/hyperalgesia), acts on EP3 receptors in the hypothalamic thermoregulatory center to elevate the fever set point (providing the mechanistic link between [[il-1beta]], COX-2, and fever), causes vasodilation and increased vascular permeability through EP2 and EP4 receptors (inflammatory edema), and promotes leukocyte recruitment. PGE2 also has immunomodulatory effects: through EP2/EP4-mediated cAMP elevation in macrophages, PGE2 suppresses [[tnf-alpha]] and [[il-1beta]] while promoting [[il-10]] production, representing a negative feedback mechanism within the inflammatory response. PGI2 contributes to vasodilation and inhibits platelet aggregation. In the context of HPA axis regulation, prostaglandins generated by COX-2 in hypothalamic and perivascular cells mediate part of the cytokine-driven stimulation of [[crh]] release, as demonstrated by the ability of NSAIDs to partially block cytokine-induced HPA axis activation.

## Holon Context

Within the [[innate-immune-response]] holon, COX-2 is the enzymatic effector that converts the transcriptional output of [[nf-kb]] activation into bioactive lipid mediators (prostanoids) that directly produce the cardinal signs of inflammation: rubor (redness, via vasodilation), calor (heat, via vasodilation and fever), dolor (pain, via nociceptor sensitization), and tumor (swelling, via increased vascular permeability). COX-2 thus occupies the position of a signal transduction converter, translating protein-based signaling (cytokine/[[nf-kb]]/gene expression) into lipid-based signaling (prostanoids acting on EP/IP/TP receptors). This lipid mediator arm provides the inflammatory response with a distinct set of effector mechanisms (paracrine signaling, rapid onset, local action) that complement the slower, systemic effects of cytokines such as [[il-6]] and [[tnf-alpha]]. The substrate dependency of COX-2 on phospholipase A2-released arachidonic acid creates an additional regulatory node where [[cortisol]] (via annexin A1 induction) can intervene, providing the [[hpa-axis]] with dual suppressive control over prostanoid production: transcriptional suppression of COX-2 via [[nf-kb]] transrepression and substrate deprivation via annexin A1/PLA2 inhibition.

## Clinical Relevance

COX-2 is one of the most pharmacologically targeted enzymes in medicine. Non-selective NSAIDs (aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac) inhibit both COX-1 and COX-2, providing anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antipyretic effects at the cost of gastrointestinal toxicity (COX-1 inhibition reduces cytoprotective prostaglandins in gastric mucosa). COX-2-selective inhibitors (celecoxib, etoricoxib, formerly rofecoxib and valdecoxib) were developed to provide anti-inflammatory efficacy with reduced GI toxicity, but the withdrawal of rofecoxib (Vioxx) in 2004 due to increased cardiovascular risk revealed that COX-2-derived PGI2 is important for cardiovascular homeostasis. Aspirin's irreversible COX-1 inhibition in platelets provides the mechanistic basis for its antiplatelet and cardioprotective effects. COX-2 overexpression is a feature of many cancers (colorectal, breast, lung, gastric), where COX-2-derived PGE2 promotes tumor cell proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, and immune evasion; epidemiological studies consistently show reduced cancer incidence with chronic NSAID use, and celecoxib is FDA-approved for familial adenomatous polyposis. Glucocorticoids (dexamethasone, prednisone) achieve powerful anti-inflammatory effects partly through COX-2 suppression, and their efficacy in conditions from asthma to rheumatoid arthritis reflects the importance of COX-2-mediated prostanoid production in these diseases. The resolution of inflammation involves a COX-2-dependent switch from pro-inflammatory prostaglandins to specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators (lipoxins, resolvins), revealing that COX-2 has both pro-inflammatory and pro-resolution functions depending on timing and substrate availability.
