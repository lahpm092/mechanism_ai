# Annexin A1

Annexin A1 (ANXA1), also known as lipocortin-1, is a 37 kDa calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding protein that serves as a critical anti-inflammatory effector of glucocorticoid signaling. The protein belongs to the annexin superfamily characterized by their ability to bind negatively charged phospholipids in a calcium-dependent manner. Annexin A1 is abundantly expressed in cells of the [[innate-immune-response]], particularly [[macrophage]]s, neutrophils, and [[dendritic-cell]]s, where it resides in the cytoplasm and can be externalized to the cell surface upon activation. Its expression is strongly induced by [[cortisol]] acting through the [[gr]] (glucocorticoid receptor), making it one of the principal mediators through which glucocorticoids exert their anti-inflammatory effects. The protein was originally identified in the 1980s as a factor responsible for the phospholipase-inhibitory activity of glucocorticoids, and subsequent decades of research have revealed its broader roles in resolution of inflammation, efferocytosis, and immune cell trafficking.

The primary anti-inflammatory mechanism of annexin A1 involves the inhibition of [[phospholipase-a2]], specifically cytosolic phospholipase A2 (cPLA2), which cleaves arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids. By sequestering membrane phospholipids and directly interacting with cPLA2, annexin A1 limits the availability of arachidonic acid as a substrate for both [[cox-2]] and lipoxygenase pathways, thereby broadly suppressing the production of pro-inflammatory eicosanoids including prostaglandins, thromboxanes, and leukotrienes. In addition to this lipid-mediated mechanism, annexin A1 also exerts anti-inflammatory effects through the formyl peptide receptor family, particularly FPR2/ALX. Binding of annexin A1 or its N-terminal peptide Ac2-26 to FPR2/ALX triggers signaling cascades that inhibit neutrophil extravasation, promote monocyte recruitment to sites of resolving inflammation, and stimulate [[macrophage]] efferocytosis of apoptotic cells. These actions position annexin A1 as a key pro-resolving mediator that actively drives the transition from inflammation to tissue repair.

## Upstream Causes

Annexin A1 expression is principally upregulated by [[cortisol]] signaling through the [[gr]]. When [[cortisol]] binds to the [[gr]], the activated receptor translocates to the nucleus and binds to glucocorticoid response elements (GREs) in the ANXA1 promoter, driving transcriptional upregulation. This mechanism represents one of the major transactivation pathways by which glucocorticoids suppress inflammation, complementing the transrepression of [[nf-kb]] and AP-1. The induction of annexin A1 by glucocorticoids occurs across multiple immune cell types, including [[macrophage]]s, [[dendritic-cell]]s, and neutrophils. Beyond transcriptional regulation, [[cortisol]] also promotes the externalization of pre-formed annexin A1 to the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane, where it can engage FPR2/ALX receptors in an autocrine and paracrine fashion. Basal annexin A1 expression is also modulated by other factors including IL-6 (via [[il-6]]), although glucocorticoid signaling remains the dominant inducer in the context of the [[hpa-axis]] and [[glucocorticoid-feedback]].

## Downstream Effects

The downstream effects of annexin A1 converge on the resolution of inflammation and the suppression of immune cell activation. Inhibition of [[phospholipase-a2]] by annexin A1 reduces arachidonic acid release, thereby diminishing substrate availability for [[cox-2]]-mediated prostaglandin synthesis and leukotriene production. This effectively limits the amplification of the inflammatory cascade at the level of lipid mediator generation. Through FPR2/ALX receptor engagement, annexin A1 inhibits neutrophil adhesion to endothelium and transendothelial migration, reducing the influx of inflammatory cells into tissues. Simultaneously, it promotes [[macrophage]] phagocytosis of apoptotic neutrophils (efferocytosis), a process critical for the non-phlogistic clearance of dying inflammatory cells. Annexin A1 signaling through FPR2/ALX also suppresses [[nf-kb]] activation in [[macrophage]]s, providing an additional mechanism for reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine production including [[il-6]], [[tnf-alpha]], and [[il-1beta]]. In [[dendritic-cell]]s, annexin A1 contributes to the suppression of maturation and antigen-presenting capacity, further dampening the transition from innate to adaptive immune responses.

## Holon Context

Within the [[innate-immune-response]] holon, annexin A1 functions as a critical endogenous brake on inflammation, operating at the molecular scale to modulate the activity of enzymes and receptors that drive inflammatory amplification. It represents the point at which neuroendocrine signals from the [[hpa-axis]] are transduced into direct molecular suppression of immune cell function. Within the [[glucocorticoid-feedback]] holon, annexin A1 acts as an effector arm of [[cortisol]]-[[gr]] signaling, translating receptor-level events into broad anti-inflammatory outcomes. The protein thereby bridges scales: at the molecular level it inhibits [[phospholipase-a2]] and [[cox-2]] enzymatic activity, at the cellular level it modulates [[macrophage]], neutrophil, and [[dendritic-cell]] behavior, and at the tissue level it promotes the resolution of inflammatory infiltrates. Annexin A1 thus exemplifies the holon principle that molecular-scale nodes can have emergent effects across multiple organizational levels, linking the [[glucocorticoid-feedback]] loop to the functional output of the [[innate-immune-response]].

## Clinical Relevance

Annexin A1 has significant clinical implications in both inflammatory disease and glucocorticoid pharmacology. Reduced annexin A1 expression or function has been observed in patients with chronic inflammatory conditions including rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and systemic lupus erythematosus, where its deficiency may contribute to the failure of endogenous anti-inflammatory mechanisms. Conversely, the therapeutic efficacy of exogenous glucocorticoids (prednisone, dexamethasone) depends in part on their ability to induce annexin A1 expression; glucocorticoid resistance in certain patient populations has been linked to impaired ANXA1 induction. Synthetic annexin A1 mimetic peptides, particularly Ac2-26, have shown anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving efficacy in preclinical models of peritonitis, ischemia-reperfusion injury, and colitis, raising the prospect of annexin A1-based therapies that could capture the anti-inflammatory benefits of glucocorticoids without the metabolic side effects of global [[gr]] activation. Annexin A1 levels have also been explored as biomarkers for glucocorticoid responsiveness in asthma and other chronic inflammatory diseases.
